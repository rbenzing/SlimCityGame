# Tips

- If a zone won't develop, check the three requirements in order: **road access,
  power, water**. The most common mistake is a utility placed a tile or two away
  from any road — move it flush against one.
- **Gravel roads carry no electricity and no water**: a dirt track has no
  cable in it and no main under it. Anything down a gravel lane stays dark
  until you run a power line to it, and neither power nor water will travel
  _through_ a gravel stretch to the paved road beyond. Houses and farms down a
  dirt road need only that power line, because each has its own well.
  Anything denser, and every shop and workshop, needs a paved street for its
  water.
- **Power lines** (Electricity) reach what a road cannot: drag a run from your
  generators out to the lot, the district or the pump that a street doesn't
  connect. A line is cheaper per tile than the cheapest road, but it charges a
  small upkeep every month for as long as it stands — so a sprawling rural grid
  is a standing bill, not a one-off. Dragging back over a run you've already
  strung costs nothing, and the bulldozer takes the wire down with a refund.
  A paved street already carries power and shows it as a pole line along its
  footway, so a line strung along one is refused ("The street already carries
  power."); a line may still cross a street.
- **A line starts at a source.** A power line must begin at a power station,
  a **Substation** (Electricity) or a line that is already live; begun
  anywhere else it is refused. A substation stands beside a street, takes
  its power from that street and is where a line out to a gravel lane, a farm
  or a pump begins. A line you strung before this rule stays live.
- **Buried Cable** (Electricity) does what a line does and cannot be seen. It
  costs eight times a line to lay and half as much to keep, runs under open
  ground and under any road, and must start at a source as a line does. Laid
  along a paved street it buries that street's supply: the poles go, a green
  transformer stands every few lots, and the street costs a little less to
  keep. Its ends show as green boxes, and a riser pole stands where it meets
  an overhead line. The underground view shows the cable beside the pipes.
- Keep commercial and industrial in proportion to residential; if a demand bar is
  empty, stop zoning that type.
- Services and parks raise happiness and land value in a radius — spread them out
  rather than clustering.
- Use the infoview lenses before expanding: fix pollution, traffic, and coverage
  gaps in what you have before painting more.
- Watch **garbage** the way you watch power and water: a landfill that fills up
  or an overwhelmed incinerator silently stops collecting its area. Check the
  trash lens, then expand the landfill or add a facility across town.
- **Sandbox / testing options** (Options menu): _"Sandbox: unlock all build
  items"_ ignores milestone locks, so any zone you paint grows at once, and
  _"Unlimited money"_ ignores funds and costs — the money readout shows ∞ and
  the budget warnings stay quiet, though cash flow is still tracked, so you
  can build anything even in the red. Handy for experimenting with a layout.
