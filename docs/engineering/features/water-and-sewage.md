# Water and sewage — technical design

- **Status:** Draft
- **Date:** 2026-09-18
- **Author:** Claude Opus 5

Epic 1 of [municipal-services.md](municipal-services.md), written against
[../../game-design/features/water-and-sewage.md](../../game-design/features/water-and-sewage.md).

## What we are building, and why now

The city draws water and nothing comes back out. This epic adds the return
half — sewage generated by every consumer, collected over the road network,
discharged into water raw or treated — and makes the water carry that
contamination back to whoever draws from it nearby. Now, because it is the
largest open loop in the simulation and the cheapest content epic to get right:
no new player verb, no new UI idiom, one new grid layer, and the right place to
prove the additive save rule before an epic needs a harder one.

**Save format: yes, additively.** One new trailing `GridState` layer,
`waterPollution` (MAP_SIZE² bytes), at `SAVE_VERSION = 12` — the pattern
`district`, `landfill` and `powerLine` already follow
([../data-model.md](../data-model.md)). **A save written before this epic loads
after it, with the new service absent rather than the save rejected**:
`deserializeGrid` defaults the layer to all-zero, so nothing is contaminated, no
intake is scaled, and the sewage the city now generates is unserviced — a
pollution pressure, not a failure. The per-building backlog is runtime state and
is not saved, the call garbage already made for trash.
**Worker protocol: yes, additively** — `SimSnapshot` gains one optional `water`
channel shaped like the existing `garbage` one, and there is **no new
`Command`**, every player action here being `placeBuilding` with a new catalog
id. See [../interfaces.md](../interfaces.md).

## The design

### Data: what is stored, what is derived

One new grid layer, deliberately **not** a `FieldId` — it spreads along water
connectivity, not by the shared diffusion kernel, so it cannot ride that
schedule. Beside it a `SewageSpec` in the shape `GarbageSpec` proved, and on
`UtilitySpec` two optional fields: `abstracts?: boolean`, meaning its `waterKL`
is drawn from adjacent water and scaled by that water's contamination, and
`treatsKL?: number`, the contaminated supply a plant restores, in the same kL.

```ts
// GridState: 0..255, meaningful only where water[i] === 1. ADDITIVE layer,
// serialized LAST in the grid save (SAVE_VERSION 12).
waterPollution: Uint8Array;

export interface SewageSpec {
  capacityKL: number; // sewage collected per day
  collectionRange: number; // road-network BFS tile radius drained
  rawFraction: number; // reaching water untreated: 1 outfall, 0.1 works
}
```

**Derived, never stored.** A building's sewage is `0.8 × waterUse`, a published
fraction of a number the catalog carries; a `sewageUse` beside it would put one
fact in thirty-eight entries and let them disagree.

### Behaviour: the path through the code

One new module, `src/sim/sewage.ts`, on the garbage cadence — the same kind of
pass, so it shares that slot rather than inventing a rhythm:

1. **Generate.** Every Active building contributes `0.8 × waterUse` to a load
   keyed by building id.
2. **Collect.** For each facility with a `SewageSpec`, walk the road network from
   the nearest road tile within 2 steps of the footprint out to
   `collectionRange`, claiming buildings in id order until `capacityKL` runs out
   — the loop [../../world-sim/services-model.md](../../world-sim/services-model.md)
   describes for garbage, on [service-capacity.md](service-capacity.md)'s walk.
3. **Discharge.** Emit `collected × rawFraction × RAW_CONTAMINATION_PER_KL` into
   `waterPollution` at the water tile adjacent to the footprint, saturating.
4. **Punish what is not collected.** A building whose load nothing claimed emits
   into the existing `Pollution` field on its own footprint, through the path in
   [../../world-sim/environmental-simulation.md](../../world-sim/environmental-simulation.md).
   No new field, no new reader; land value and happiness respond for free.
5. **Spread, then decay.** One multi-source BFS across connected water, seeded
   from every discharge at once, writing `max(existing, emit × (1 − hops / 25))`
   and stopping at 25 hops — one fill for all discharges, never leaving water —
   then multiply every water tile by 255/256.

Then, in the utility pass in `src/sim/network.ts`, before supply and demand are
totalled: an abstracting facility delivers `waterKL × (1 −
waterPollution[intakeTile] / 255)`, and treatment plants the same network
reaches restore `min(remaining treatsKL, waterKL − delivered)` on top, drawn
down in building-id order for determinism. Everything after — propagation, the
shortage cut order, abandonment — is untouched.

### The placement gate

`requiresAdjacent` becomes `'rail' | 'water'`. The shape is already right: it
names a thing the footprint must touch, the check is the same orthogonal-
neighbour scan against a different grid layer, and widening a union on an
optional field is additive for TypeScript and for the save, the catalog being
data rather than saved state. `'water'` goes on both pumping facilities and all
three drainage facilities — **everything that abstracts or discharges stands on
a shore** — one rule instead of two, and what makes waterfront land contested.
The treatment plant does not take it; it sits on the supply main, the road. The
world model has no fresh/salt distinction, so `'water'` is any tile below sea
level and a coastal city may drink the sea: stated, not fixed. A 1×1 pond would
otherwise feed a 2,560 kL works, so the gate also requires **64 connected tiles**
of waterbody — 2.6 ha, which at 1 m of drawdown is 26 ML, about three days of
the largest intake. The arithmetic is real; the three days are **our assumption,
not a standard**.

### Deriving the buildings

A process utility is not sized by floor area — it is tanks and clarifiers on
open ground, which is what [../../art/civic-massing.md](../../art/civic-massing.md)
means by putting the process vessel outside the building. The method therefore
runs the other way round from a school: sum the vessels from published design
rates, add circulation, convert at 400 m² per tile, pick a height from what the
plant is, then run the massing formula as a check on the box the renderer draws.
Rates, all conventional, at 1 ML/d = 41.7 m³/h: primary settlement **1.2 m/h**,
final clarifier **0.8 m/h**, aeration **8 h**, potable sedimentation
**1.5 m/h**, filtration **5 m/h**, flocculation and chlorine contact **30 min**,
clearwater storage **4 h**. So at 2.4 ML/d = 100 m³/h: primary 100 ÷ 1.2 =
83 m², aeration 800 m³ at 4 m = 200 m², final 100 ÷ 0.8 = 125 m²; at 8 ML/d,
278, 593 and 417 m². The ×1.6 circulation multiplier is **our assumption**.

| Entry                    | Process / site area                                                            | Tiles | Footprint | Height   | Formula GFA | Enclosed floor |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----- | --------- | -------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| `water-pump-small`       | 140 pump hall + 48 wet well + 122 access + 30 parking = **340 m²**             | 0.85  | **2×1**   | **8 m**  | 925 m²      | 140 m²         |
| `water-pump-large`       | 200 plate + 168 intake + 167 balancing + 244 access + 206 parking = **985 m²** | 2.5   | **3×2**   | **12 m** | 4,163 m²    | 400 m²         |
| `water-treatment`        | 278 + 208 + 60 + 70 + 417 + 150 + 250 = 1,433, ×1.6 = **2,293 m²**             | 5.7   | **3×2**   | **10 m** | 3,469 m²    | 300 m²         |
| `sewage-outlet`          | 24 screen + 24 grit + 20 kiosk + 60 headwall + 122 access = **250 m²**         | 0.63  | **1×1**   | **4 m**  | 231 m²      | 44 m²          |
| `sewage-treatment-small` | 60 + 83 + 200 + 125 + 150 + 180 = 798, ×1.6 = **1,277 m²**                     | 3.2   | **2×2**   | **9 m**  | 2,081 m²    | 180 m²         |
| `sewage-treatment-large` | 140 + 278 + 593 + 417 + 500 + 225 = 2,153, ×1.6 = **3,445 m²**                 | 8.6   | **3×3**   | **14 m** | 7,284 m²    | 450 m²         |

The formula GFA exceeds the enclosed floor deliberately: it is the box the
renderer draws, and for a works that box is digesters and clarifiers, not
storeys. Both are printed so nobody reads 7,284 m² as a claim about offices;
only the outfall agrees, because it is nothing but its chamber. **Two overrides,
stated.** A planning figure of 0.25 ha per ML/d would put the treatment plant at
62 tiles against the vessel sum's 6 — buffer land and perimeter, not building —
so we take the vessel sum. And the works must read as different buildings, which
2×2 at 9 m against 3×3 at 14 m does.

### The rules the implementation must satisfy

1. A building's sewage is `0.8 × waterUse`, never stored; what no facility
   collects emits Pollution on that footprint and never abandons it.
2. A facility collects only from buildings reachable over the road network
   within `collectionRange`, in building-id order, up to `capacityKL`.
3. Contamination reaches only tiles where `water === 1`, connected to the
   discharge, within 25 hops, falling linearly to zero — so two waterbodies
   split by one land tile never share it — and decays by 255/256 per pass.
4. An abstracting facility delivers `waterKL × (1 − contamination / 255)` read
   at its intake — 20% of rating 5 tiles below a raw outfall, 100% at 30 — and
   treatment plants on the same network restore that up to their summed
   `treatsKL` and no further.
5. Placement refuses a site with no adjacent water, and refuses a 1×1 pond.
6. A save at version ≤ 11 loads with `waterPollution` all-zero, every
   pre-existing facility behaving identically, and nothing abandoned.

## What could go wrong

**The fill is the whole map where the map is mostly ocean.** A multi-source BFS
over connected water can touch tens of thousands of tiles; the 25-hop cap bounds
each source, but sources merge. Mitigation: one fill for all discharges, on the
garbage cadence, with the visited set a `Uint8Array` stamp. The gate is
[../performance-budget.md](../performance-budget.md); if it does not fit, the
fallback is a lower cadence, not a smaller radius.

**The additive rule bites hardest here.** A loaded city instantly generates
sewage it has no facility for. Had that been an abandonment pressure, every
existing save would degrade on upgrade — which is why it is a pollution
pressure, and why rule 6 is written first.

**There is no downstream**, because
[../../world-sim/world-model.md](../../world-sim/world-model.md) has no flow, so
a player reasoning about rivers the way rivers work will be wrong and the UI
must not imply a current. **And the catalog is 3.1× light on per-capita water**,
left alone deliberately with every capacity derived through that one factor:
until it is retuned, the litres here and the kL in the catalog are different
litres — a trap unless it stays written down.

## Alternatives

**Contamination as `FieldId` member 9.** Rejected. Every `FieldId` field shares
one kernel, one schedule, one save block and one lens shape; this shares none, so
it would be special-cased out of the shared pass on arrival — and it changes
`FIELD_COUNT`, baked into the save's field block and the `requestField`/`field`
messages. A trailing grid layer is the smaller contract change, and costs 64 KB
on a grid [../data-model.md](../data-model.md) already calls a few MB, mostly
zero and nearly free after compression.

**Contamination as the existing `Pollution` field.** The load-bearing rejection:
Pollution diffuses with a 4-neighbour blend that knows nothing about the water
mask, so sewage would climb the bank and settle in the suburbs. Wrong
behaviour, not merely imprecise.

**A sparse `Map` over water tiles, or a magnitude on the `water` mask.**
Rejected: the map is cheaper in bytes but needs a fallback branch in every
reader, its own save encoder and a second path to a texture, and widening
`water` from 0/1 makes every `water[i] === 1` test wrong. **A real flow model**,
giving a true downstream, is rejected too — the world model excludes dynamic
flow deliberately, for cost.

**Sewage as a sixth `ServiceKind` with a coverage field.** Rejected: it is a
collected volume, like garbage, not a radiating quality — and would bring a
funding slider pretending a works runs at half flow for half price.

## How we will know it works

Every numbered rule above is a test, and rule 6 — the pre-epic save — is written
first, because it protects every existing city. Two more: a 1,000-person city
with no drainage accumulates sewage and abandons nothing, and the collection
pass rides the epic-0 walk rather than adding a second.

**This epic renders, so read-backs are not enough.** Looked at in a browser:

1. **The outfall at the river**, default pitch: the 1×1 headwall reads as a
   headwall and not a grey box, with the plume visibly centred on it.
2. **The edge of the plume**, 25 tiles out: the tint reaches clean water without
   banding or a hard ring, over the animated surface, not a still frame.
3. **Two waterbodies split by a ridge**, one contaminated: the far one must look
   clean. A read-back of rule 3 passes while the shader samples the wrong thing.
4. **The two works side by side** — 2×2 at 9 m against 3×3 at 14 m — reading as
   different buildings, digesters correct against the person and car anchors.
5. **A pumping works on the shoreline**: the body sits on the waterline without
   clipping into the water or floating above it. Arithmetic cannot tell us this.
6. **The `'sewage'` lens** over a city with one outfall and one works: the two
   plumes must be distinguishable on the ramp, not both simply "bad".

## Out of scope

- Water flow, currents, floods, any notion of downstream, and a fresh/salt
  distinction — and therefore desalination.
- Water quality beyond one scalar: no nitrates, temperature, dissolved oxygen;
  and no pipe network separate from the road, water still travelling the road.
- Illness from contaminated supply (epic 2) and sewage as a recoverable resource
  (epic 5).
- Retuning `waterUse` to the real per-capita figure — a separate, deliberate
  change; see [../../DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).
