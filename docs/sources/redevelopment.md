# Buildings rise with the town — sources

What was found for [the spec](../specs/redevelopment.md), and what was not. A figure marked _summary_ was read as a search result's summary of the page, not on the page.

## How tall, for how large a town

No source ties a building's storeys to its town's population, so the ceilings are dials.

- **Floors.** Of 5.92 million US commercial buildings, 4,106 thousand have one floor, 1,286 thousand two, 370 thousand three, 142 thousand four to nine and 16 thousand ten or more: 0.27% ([CBECS 2018, table B1](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/commercial/data/2018/bc/html/b1.php), read on the page). Ten floors and over hold 6.2% of the floor space.
- **Small-town limits.** A small town's centre is commonly limited to 45 ft, three or four storeys: Bountiful UT, Vancouver WA's Main Street, Dover NH (_summary_; the towns' populations were not read).
- **City size and towers.** The count of tall buildings per head rises with city size, with an elasticity of 0.27 above a million people (Ahlfeldt and Barr, _The economics of skyscrapers_, 2022; _summary_). It gives no threshold for a town of thousands.

## How fast buildings are replaced

No current annual rate was found, so no rate is set: a building is rebuilt when its level-up conditions hold, as before.

- Homes are lost at about 0.45% a year (a trade body's reading of HUD's inventory-change tables for 2011 to 2013; _summary_).
- The median US commercial building is 36 years old (CBECS 2018, by a secondary account; _summary_).
- Buildings are demolished for the land's value and for being unsuitable, not for wearing out; most steel and concrete buildings demolished in one city were under 50 years old (O'Connor, 2004; _summary_).

## Parking built into a building

- **Space and height.** 325 sq ft a space is a good structure and 350 a poorer one; a parking level is 10.5 to 11.5 ft floor to floor (trade and precast-industry articles; _summary_). The game's garages already use 30 m² and 3.35 m ([structured parking](../specs/structured-parking.md)).
- **Ramps.** A ramp with stalls on it is held to 1 in 15; a ramp with none runs at 10 to 15% (a city's parking-structure guidelines citing the building code; _summary_). The ramp to a level below carries no stalls, so 1 in 8.
- **Clearance.** 7 ft in traffic areas (Massachusetts 780 CMR 406; _summary_); 98 in where a van-accessible space is served ([2010 ADA Standards 502.5](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/chapter/ch05/)). The portal is 2.5 m clear.
- **Entrance.** A two-way garage drive is 20 ft in Alhambra CA, Evanston IL and Colorado Springs, 22 to 28 ft elsewhere (_summary_).
- **The street face.** Codes that allow ground-floor parking ask for other uses between it and the street: New York wraps it 30 ft deep, Cleveland 15 ft, Columbus 12 ft (_summary_). The game's rule that a garage's street face is a shopfront or a lobby is the same idea.

Not found: the building code's own text for ramps and clear height; how many levels below ground are typical; any rule for when a town's first tower appears.
