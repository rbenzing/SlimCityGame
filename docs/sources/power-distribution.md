# Power distribution — derivations and sources

The research behind [the spec](../specs/power-distribution.md). A figure marked _read_ was read in its source; one marked _relayed_ came through a summary of it and wants checking before it is leaned on harder.

## The pole

- A pole is set a tenth of its length plus 2 ft into the ground. A 40 ft pole is set 6 ft and stands 34 ft, 10.4 m; a 35 ft pole stands 29.5 ft, 9.0 m. — [PRE-CNS-002, Distribution Pole Setting Guidelines](https://precorp.coop/sites/precorp/files/Standards/0-Process%20Standards/PRE-CNS-002%20Distribution%20Pole%20Setting%20and%20Soil%20Probe%20Usage%20Guidelines.pdf), _relayed_; California sets the same pole 5.5 ft in firm soil, [GO 95 Rule 49.1-C](https://ia.cpuc.ca.gov/gos/resmajor/DesNo14-02-015/DesNo14-02-015-Rule049_1C.htm).
- Stock sizes are 35 and 40 ft in classes 1 to 5; a 40 ft pole measures 31 to 41 in round the butt. — [PPL, Sketch 47](https://www.pplelectric.com/-/media/PPLElectric/At-Your-Service/Docs/REMSI/Sketches/sketch47-print.pdf), [Ameren, 02 Poles](https://ameren.com/-/media/corporate-site/files/safetypartners/dist-construction-standards/02-poles.ashx), _relayed_.
- Poles stand about 125 ft apart in towns (100 to 150 ft) and 250 to 300 ft in the country. — [EEPower, Primary distribution systems, part 3](https://eepower.com/technical-articles/primary-distribution-systems-part-3-overhead-and-underground-design-choices/), _relayed_.

On a 20 m tile a town span is two tiles, 131 ft, and a country span four, 262 ft.

## Where a pole stands

- On a kerbed street a pole stands at least 2 ft behind the kerb's face, "at or near the right-of-way line and outside of the sidewalk"; in a median, at least 30 ft from the travelled way; on a rural road 30 ft from it, and 20 ft on an unkerbed urban one. — [Hawaii Administrative Rules 19-105-15](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-19-105-15), _read_.
- At 40 mph or less a pole stands 5 ft behind the kerb, and 1.5 ft where there is no room; elsewhere 6 ft from the shoulder and 2 ft up the backslope of a ditch. — [Maine DOT, 17-229 CMR ch. 210 §11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/maine/17-229-C-M-R-ch-210-SS-11), _read_.
- Florida asks 4 ft on a kerbed road of 45 mph or less. — [FDOT Utility Bulletin 03-16-2010](https://www.fdot.gov/docs/default-source/content-docs/programmanagement/utilities/docs/bulletins/UB-03-16-2010.pdf), _relayed_.
- New above-ground lines go as far from the travelled way as they can, by preference on the right-of-way line. Along a freeway a line is allowed only in a strip outside the access control, and a crossing keeps as far from the roadway as it can. — [23 CFR 645.209](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/23/645.209), _read_.

The game takes Hawaii's 2 ft, 0.6 m, as the least distance behind a kerb, and the outer edge of the footway as the place. No source gave a distance from a drive or a corner, and none described a pole line in an alley; both are dials.

## Clearances

- Wires hang about 15.5 ft over a road, a street or an alley, and 12 ft over ground only people walk on. — NESC Table 232-1 as utilities publish it: [UPPCO, clearances](https://www.uppco.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/ESRM-UPPCO_new_sect2_clearances-8.17.22.pdf), [Columbus, service drop clearance](https://new.columbus.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/1/utilities/documents/power-tdmis/606-service-drop-cable-clearance-for-conductors.pdf), _relayed_.
- Communication cables hang 40 in below the power on a shared pole. — [IAEI Magazine, on NESC 235 and 238](https://iaeimagazine.org/?p=8876), _relayed_.

## The cross-country line

- A 69 kV line on single poles takes a 70 ft right of way, 35 ft a side; its poles stand 70 to 80 ft and 300 to 400 ft apart. — [Great River Energy, project fact sheet](https://greatriverenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/ScenicProjectFactsheet.pdf), _relayed_; other filings give 100 ft and 65 to 90 ft.
- For scale above it: a 230 kV line stands 70 to 95 ft on spans of 700 to 900 ft. — [WAPA, engineering description](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090508045138/http:/www.wapa.gov/transmission/eptp/pdf/Engineering.pdf), _relayed_.

A tile is 66 ft wide, so the line's corridor is one tile; a 100 m span is 328 ft, and a 21 m pole is 70 ft.

## The substation

- A distribution substation carries 5 to 60 MVA, commonly two transformers each able to carry the whole load. — [Electrical Engineering Portal, Distribution substation](https://electrical-engineering-portal.com/distribution-substation), _relayed_.
- One 25 MVA transformer with its switchgear and 69 kV bus fills about half an acre inside the fence. — [California CEQAnet, project 2009062051](https://ceqanet.lci.ca.gov/Project/2009062051), _relayed_.
- A 69 to 138 kV distribution substation costs $3 million to $15 million with its land; $4,600 to $18,500 an MVA. — MISO's transmission cost guide as [relayed here](https://www.irecruit.co/guides/substation-construction-cost), _relayed_.
- At a plant, a step-up transformer raises the generator's voltage to the line's, one switchyard bay for each. — [TCE, generator step-up transformer case study](https://www.tataconsultingengineers.com/case_study/case-study-generator-step-up-transformer-gsu-configuration/), _read_.

Half an acre is 2,023 m²; two tiles by two is 1,600 m², 0.4 acre. No source gave homes served by a substation or a feeder, so the substation limits nothing.

The game's price is the least of that range, $3 million, at the rate its wind turbine sets: about $1.9 million for one megawatt at ¢190, ¢100 to the $1 million, so ¢300. Its upkeep of ¢6 a month is a dial: two parts in a hundred, a little over the turbine's, for equipment nobody attends.

## Boxes on the surface

- Pad-mounted switchgear stands about 45 in high on a 6 in base, 75 in wide and 70 to 73 in long. — [Ameren, standard 53](https://ameren.com/-/media/corporate-site/files/safetypartners/dist-construction-standards/53-equipment-switch.ashx), _relayed_.
- A house's pad-mounted transformer is about 40 by 36 in, and 32 in high. — [Dominion, residential equipment guide](https://www.dominionenergy.com/-/media/content/start-stop-service/new-construction/pdfs/global/residential-customer-equipment-guide.pdf), [Ameren, pad-mounted equipment](https://ameren.com/missouri/company/reliability/inspections/pad-mounted-equipment), _relayed_.
- Keep 10 ft clear in front of the doors and 3 ft at the back and sides. — [Idaho Power, clearance requirements](https://www.idahopower.com/accounts-service/construction-remodeling/construction-clearance-requirements/), _read_.
- The colour is Munsell 7GY 3.29/1.5, pad-mount green. — [Eversource, M3902](https://www.eversource.com/Content/docs/default-source/ema---pdfs/m3902.pdf), _relayed_.
- Where a cable comes up a pole, it climbs in conduit under a guard. — [Clark PUD, standard 1300](https://www.clarkpublicutilities.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/1300-Underground-Risers-Cables-Connectors-1.pdf), _relayed_.
- A pole at an angle or a dead end is held by a down guy and an anchor. — [Clark PUD, standard 0700](https://www.clarkpublicutilities.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/0700-Guys-Anchors.pdf), _relayed_.
- A city centre has no poles: Manhattan's supply is wholly underground, with 246,000 manholes and service boxes for 2.5 million customers. — [T&D World, Con Edison secures safety underground](https://www.tdworld.com/features/con-edison-secures-safety-underground-system), _read_.

Sizes for a junction cabinet smaller than switchgear, for a handhole and for the spacing of manholes were not found.

## Burial

- Primary cable lies 36 in down, and the secondary, a service and a street light's feed 24 in. — [FPL underground distribution manual, as filed](https://www.floridapsc.com/library/FILINGS/2016/07490-2016/Support/254%20Attachment%202%20DERM%20Section%205.3.1.pdf), _relayed_; one city asks 48 in for primary conduit.

## What it costs to build

- Overhead averages $120,000 a mile; underground $700,000 to over $1.5 million. — the Edison Electric Institute's study as [summarised here](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/grid-modernization/underground-distribution-attractive-but-difficult-to-justify/), _read_ in summary; a later round gives $0.3 million to $4.5 million by density, [Underground Infrastructure](https://undergroundinfrastructure.com/magazine/2013/december-2013-vol-68-no-12/features/despite-higher-costs-electric-utilities-increasingly-favor-undergrounding).

The ratio runs from 6 to 12. The game takes 8.

## What it costs to keep

- Utilities replace 2.7% of their poles a year in one filing and about 3% in another; a rate under 0.5% was called unsustainable. — [Hydro One, interrogatory response](https://www.hydroone.com/abouthydroone/RegulatoryInformation/JointRateApplications/Documents/HONI_19_PWU_IRR_20211129.pdf), _relayed_.
- A pole is depreciated over 35 years, lasts 40 to 50 in one utility's account, and is claimed to pass 70 when maintained. — _relayed_, and the three do not agree.
- Burying a pole line avoids tree trimming and pole inspection worth $10,400 to $11,300 a mile, about $370 a mile a year. — Florida Power & Light before its commission, in [PURC, collaborative research report](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/centers/purc/docs/papers/energy/hurricane-hardening-papers/PURC_Collaborative_Research_Report_02_14_08.pdf), _relayed_.
- Underground has about half the outages and each lasts about 58% longer; faults are harder to find. — three North Carolina utilities, in the same Edison Electric Institute summary, _relayed_.
- Cable life past 40 years is not established; injecting an old cable costs about a third of replacing it and buys some 25 years. — [EPRI, 2025 slide](https://distribution.epri.com/underground/img/public/2025/Spring_2025_Sub_A.pdf), _relayed_; [Seattle City Light](https://powerlines.seattle.gov/2015/10/01/city-light-plans-silicone-cable-injection-in-parts-of-service-territory/), _read_.

What the sources carry is a build ratio and a routine-upkeep saving. They do not carry "a cable outlasts a pole", so the game does not model one. No citable figure was found for the installed cost of replacing one pole.
