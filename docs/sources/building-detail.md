# Building detail: reference library, slice records and lessons

The research and per-kit records behind [the building-detail spec](../specs/building-detail.md). The library names, for each kind, the real US type it is drawn against, the features that identify it at about 40 m, and the measured figures its parts take. A figure with no source is marked as the game's own. Figures tagged single-source or vendor stay so until a slice finds a better one; the slice's art document says which it used. Each slice's art document restates the dimensions it uses and nothing here is repeated there.

## The measurement and the performance slice

The first slice built the measurement before any recipe existed. The art bible's old figure was under a hundred triangles an assembly; the water tower is about 2,500. The standard had said a kit part is a 12-triangle box.

- The measurement found the frame already at 23–38 ms on a laptop GPU before any detail, an overview at 4,118 draw calls, and yards costing more than houses. The figures are in [modeling-standards](../art/modeling-standards.md#what-the-measurement-read).
- The player chose a tiered, measured budget, and once the measurement had counted chose that a tier counts everything on the lot: body, roof, parts, paving, yard, trees, cars and interments. The tiers are set where the shipped buildings were found.
- No save-sized city existed and growth is tick-driven, so `tools/frame-budget.mjs` builds a synthetic Metropolis by command. Sandbox mode makes the place command take any catalog entry; the script lays streets over dry ground and places about nine thousand zoned buildings in the catalog's real mix plus one of every kit, in about eighty seconds, with the sim paused (a running sim clears a city with no power or zones).
- It reads frame time with the browser's frame cap off, and draw calls and triangles from `window.__slimcity.renderInfo()`, over four fixed views by day and night against the empty map, and writes the record beside the shots.

The performance slice paid for the detail before it was drawn, and the houses built to the improved frame.

- **The overview's draw calls were the lots.** `src/render/lots.ts` added a `THREE.Mesh` per building for its paving, so the overview drew thousands where the oblique's frustum held a few hundred; terrain and roads were already one mesh per 16-tile chunk (`src/render/terrain.ts`, `src/render/roadsmesh.ts`). The lot renderer took the road renderer's shape: one merged mesh per chunk on the one lot material, a chunk rebuilt when a lot in it is added, changed or removed. A test asserts many lots in one chunk hold one mesh, and that removing a lot rebuilds only its chunk.
- **The yard.** A broadleaf was 192 triangles (three seven-by-five sphere blobs), a bush 56, a pool about 160, a trampoline up to 360, under every yard and across every forest. The species took the ceilings in [vegetation](../visual-render/vegetation.md#trees) (broadleaf 100, pine 40, poplar 50, shrub 30) and the yard parts those in [buildings](../art/buildings/residential-lots.md) (pool under 60, trampoline under 110); a home on a normal lot landed under 900 triangles and on an acre under 1,500. The fence's panels (up to 80 on an acre, twelve triangles each) are cut at 5 m and drawn without their underside, and the grill's legs lose theirs. Built figures: a home on a normal lot is 894 triangles at most and on an acre 1,370.
- **The solar farm.** It was 9,000 triangles: 34 post boxes and 10 seam boxes a row. A row is now a frame-coloured slab, the panel and the module seam as quads, on one line of two-sided posts every 6 m: 84 triangles. The chain-link fence round the lot takes a post every 10 m. The farm is 1,944, held under 2,000.
- **The result.** Draw calls fell by half to two-thirds and every view shed 10 M triangles; the frame fell 2–3 ms at the street and held on the overview, which is triangle-bound on the measured GPU. The overview still draws three times the oblique's calls; that, and the triangles the city adds over the empty map, are the next performance work, logged in the roadmap.
- `tools/frame-budget.mjs` ran again on the same seed and views, three interleaved runs of each build. A cheaper CPU side lets the browser queue frames, so the tool reports the 95th percentile of eight-frame averages, not single-frame percentiles. The shots were looked at: the lots are identical, and the trees, bushes, pools and trampolines read at the street.

## Per-slice records

Each slice's dimensions live in the art document it names; this is what each part list and its tests hold.

### Houses

The house kit in `src/render/houses.ts` already was the part pool the plan described: a `HousePart` union with one `InstancedSlotPool` per part (roof, garage, carport, door, fence, pool, trampoline, grill, bush, tree), placed from the pure `HouseLotPlan` in `src/render/houselot.ts`. Spec: [the house kit's parts](../art/buildings/residential-kinds/the-house-kits-parts.md).

- `HousePart` gains `chimney`, `stoop`, `portico`, `porch`, `porchRoof`, `dormer`, `shutter`, `garageLeaf` and `parapet`, each one low-poly geometry on the kit material (the roof's day/night tint).
- The roof prism overhangs the walls by the eave and rake figures; pitch is 5:12 for level 1 (the existing 0.42), 8:12 for levels 2 and 3, under the 4.5 m rise cap. The pitch is a share of the half-span, fixed by level; the ridge runs along the frontage.
- `houselot.ts` grows the plan with the stoop or porch at the door, the chimney's plan, the dormers' positions and the parapets at a row's boundaries: pure, seeded, in metres.
- `windowGrid(entry, tierHeight)` in `src/render/facade.ts` is the function the material's own grid size comes from, so `houselot.ts` places shutters on the facade's windows.
- Wall colours stay the facade's (the catalog colour desaturated with a hue jitter, mirrored in the shader, so the survey's wall weights are not applied); `trimColor(seed, wall)` in `houses.ts` picks the one trim colour a house at the survey weights, white whenever the wall is dark, for the shutters, fascia, garage leaf and door.
- Ceilings in `geometrybudget.test.ts`: 1,300 (normal lot) and 1,800 (acre); the house tier stays 3,000.
- Tests (`houses.test.ts`, `houselot.test.ts`): the three levels carry the three houses' parts; a ranch has no shutters and a Cape two dormers; a colonial's chimney stands at a gable end and clears the ridge by 0.6 m; a porch is 1.8 m deep and its floor 0.6 m up on three risers; a shutter pair tiles its bay; a row has parapets at its two boundaries; parts follow the body through construction (hidden at the construction scale as the roof is), abandonment (kept) and removal (freed); every part inside the lot at all four street sides; determinism.
- Shots, `tools/house-shots.mjs`: a street of ranches, of Capes and of colonials beside the 4 m car; a duplex, a fourplex and a townhouse row; the same at night; a close shot of a porch and a stoop beside the 1.75 m car-to-roof read (no pedestrian hook exists).

### Storefronts

The commercial kit in `src/render/buildingkit.ts` is the part pool for the blocks: one `InstancedSlotPool` per part kind, placed by `computePartPlacements` against the road-facing wall `findRoadFacingEdge` finds, skipped without a frontage, drawn only while a building is Active. The `buildingparts.ts` of the first plan was not needed. Spec: [the storefront kit's parts](../art/buildings/commercial-kinds/the-storefront-kits-parts.md).

- `PART` kinds gain awning, bulkhead, cornice, stripCanopy, stripColumn, anchorParapet, pylon, entryTower, brickBase, cartCorral, menuBoard, clearanceBar, pickupCanopy, dumpsterEnclosure, fuelFascia, fuelIsland, dispenser, bollard, priceSign, lobbyPortal, roofScreen, coolingTower, porteCochere, rooftopUnit, roofHatch, and two the lit material needs apart from the unlit body: signPole (under a pylon, price sign, menu board, clearance bar or corral sign) and towerGlazing (the supermarket tower's lit glazing). anchorParapet also stands the supermarket's stepped parapet. The unit-box pools stay.
- `kitLightMaterial` is on the pattern of `stationLightMaterial` in `utilitykits.ts`: emissive white driven by the night factor, carrying the sign bands, the pylon and price-sign faces, the supermarket tower's glazing and the office lobby portal.
- Rooftop units are placed from the top tier's rect (`massing.ts` `computeSetbacks`), one per 232 m² of its plate, at most eight, spaced on the long axis, 2 m inside the parapet, plus a hatch; the office tower takes a screen and a cooling tower instead.
- The drive-through lane is paving, so it belongs to the lot layout: `src/shared/lotlayout.ts` gains a `driveThrough` path for a restaurant whose lot leaves a 3.66 m lane room along the far side of the body from the drive to the pickup wall; `lots.ts` lays it in the darker drive colour in the chunk mesh; the kit places the board, bar, canopy and enclosure on that path and places nothing where there is none.
- The strip's tenant bays are the frontage cut into bays of about 8 m from the body width, the anchor two bays at the end away from the drive; the pylon stands at the lot's drive mouth 1.5 m inside the street edge.
- Tests (`archetypes.test.ts`, `buildingkit.test.ts`): each kind's part list; every part against the road-facing wall or on the roof, inside the lot, at all four street sides; the awning's underside at 2.45 m and the canopy's at 2.75 m; the pylon 6.1 m tall and 1.5 m inside the lot line; the supermarket tower at 1.28 × the body; the unit count per roof area and its cap; the drive-through present on a lot with room and absent without; lit parts on the lit material and nothing else; parts freed on removal and absent while Constructing; determinism; the block tier.
- Shots, `tools/storefront-shots.mjs`: a Main Street of shops, a strip with its pylon, a supermarket from its car park, a drive-through from above, a filling station, an office and a hotel downtown, beside the 4 m car; the same at night, where sign bands, the pylon and the lobby glow in their own warm-shifted livery, the storefront glazing is lit from inside, and the rest does not glow.
- `tools/nightlook-shots.mjs` measures the night look: a colonial and a shop from one pinned camera at noon, 03:00 and midnight, a wall, a shutter and a roof pixel read back, failing outside the 30–50% body band or on a pane that clips or is not warm ([lighting](../visual-render/lighting.md)).

### Industry and farms

Industry extends the commercial kit as the storefronts did; the farms extend `src/render/farms.ts` from the plan in `src/render/farmlot.ts`, which already draws the barn, house, silos, bins, orchard and paddock. Specs: [the industrial kit's parts](../art/buildings/industrial-kinds/the-industrial-kits-parts.md), [the farmstead's parts](../art/buildings/industrial-kinds/the-farmsteads-parts.md).

- `PART` kinds gain wainscot, walkDoor, gutter, downspout, ridgeVent, dockShelter, dockBumper, dockLight, officeCorner, officeCanopy, panelReveal, parapetBand, clerestory, entryCanopy, condenser, bundWall, bullet, pipeRack, processColumn, furnace, stove, skipBridge, duct, chipPile, stacker; loadingDock, rollUpDoors, monitorRoof, roofArray and tanks stay. Industrial parts get a per-kind table like `STOREFRONT_PARTS`.
- `kitPlanFor` carries the lot plan; the dock bank reads the plan's loading berths and stands on that side, the office corner on the street face. `lotlayout.ts` stripes trailer stalls 3.66 × 16.8 m in the truck court where the berths are. Without berths the dock keeps the street face.
- The office corner's glazing and the factory's clerestory go on `kitLightMaterial`.

## What could go wrong, as designed for

- **The frame.** Ten thousand bodies each carrying six parts is sixty thousand slots across a few dozen buckets: a few dozen draw calls, inside the stated ~300; the vertex count is what the measurement decides. If the house tier does not hold at the Metropolis, the recipe loses its smallest parts first (shutters, sills) and keeps its silhouette (roof, porch, garage).
- **Two systems drawing one building.** The body instancer and the part pool must agree on position, rotation, construction scale and lifecycle tint, or a porch floats off an abandoned house; a test asserts a part follows its body through construction, abandonment and bulldoze.
- **Bodies sized by the sim.** A house body is 4.75 m per lot tile each way and a block 13.6 m, and both feed homes and floor in the design docs. Parts stand on and beside the body; nothing here moves a wall.
- **The reference is single-source.** Several figures are one permit drawing or a vendor page. Each slice says which it used; a better source replaces it in that slice's document, not silently in code.
- **The old kits.** Redoing the coal plant or the sewage works changes geometry the cemetery, crematorium and station tests do not touch, but the kit registry test and the coexistence test do; they change by addition only.

## How each slice was proven

1. The slice's section in the art bible first, every dimension sourced or stated as the game's own.
2. The recipe and its parts, pure layout from footprint, lot, level and seed.
3. Tests: deterministic; every part inside its lot at all four rotations; parts clear of each other and of the body's door and drive; the stated dimensions; the triangle tier; night material only on glazing.
4. A shots script: each kind at every level at the default pitch beside the 4 m car, a street of the kind for the variation read, and the same at night; every shot looked at against the reference.
5. The frame test at the Metropolis, before and after, in the PR.

The first-town test is untouched throughout: nothing here touches growth.

## Lessons the programme left

- A tier counts everything on the lot, never the building alone: yards cost more than houses, so counting only the body hid the real cost.
- A cheaper CPU side makes single-frame percentiles lie; read windowed averages.
- The first plan named a `buildingparts.ts` pool; the houses and the storefronts each found the existing kit pool (`houses.ts`, `buildingkit.ts`) was the right place, so extend the kit that is there.
