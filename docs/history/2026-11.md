### A median is one island with a nose (built 2026-11-13)

The player doubted the medians, and the review bore it out: an avenue's
median stopped flat against the crosswalk with no nose, was cut square at

- **A nose at every end.** A half-circle of the island's half-width with a
  yellow kerb (MUTCD 3J.04), behind the stop line at a junction, at a dead
  end, and where the next road has no median.
- **Its lines.** Solid yellow runs both sides (3J.05) and ends where the
  nose begins. No line changes colour along a road.
- **Avenue to one-way.** The lines crossed where the two met. The stored
  flow says which carriageway carries on; the rest close to their own
  kerb, and none crosses another.
- **Checked.** No wild tree stands in a median: the trees there are the
  avenue's own planting. Whether a 1.2 m median should carry trees is on
  the defects list.

### One palette number is one colour (built 2026-11-13)

Roofs, back fences, sound walls, trampolines and shop canopies read
near-black in daylight, beside roads and grass that read right. The first
attempt raised the dark swatches and the ambient light and got a charcoal
roof from 9 to about 40 against a road at 149. Its measurements found the
cause instead.

- **Two paths.** A swatch used as a vertex colour reached the renderer as
  its number ÷ 255. The same swatch handed over as a hex was decoded from
  sRGB first and arrived two to seven times darker, where the tone
  mapping's toe crushed it. Roads and terrain are vertex colour; roofs,
  walls and fences are not.
- **One conversion.** `paletteHex` makes the hex whose decoded value is the
  number ÷ 255, and every palette hex goes through it `[R-render-040]`. A
  part that glows keeps its old value through `litHex`, so lit windows and
  signs read as before: the night gauge counts the same lit pixels.
- **Readings at noon.** Charcoal roof 9 to about 100, fence 25 to 84,
  manhole cover 10 to 65, trampoline mat near-black to 55, sound wall 25 to
  83, with the road at 149.
- **What else moved.** Shade takes more sky and ground bounce (day
  hemisphere 0.9 to 1.4; night and dusk 0.5 to 0.75, holding the night
  bands). An abandoned building is half its active reading, not a quarter,
  from one table. Manhole covers take palette colours. The home fence takes
  the shared night-tinted material. Charcoal shingle is 50, not 40.
- **`nightlook-shots` passes**, untouched; it had failed on the black roof
  by design.

### Undoing an edit puts the funds back exactly (built 2026-11-12)

A bulldoze refunded half and its undo charged the full price, so a mistake
undone cost half the building; a placement's undo, a bulldoze, gave back
only half. The player decided undo costs nothing `[R-architecture-008]`.

- **A settle ends every inverse.** The worker appends a new command,
  `settle`, carrying the opposite of what the batch cost; a batch that ends
  in one costs exactly that in all, whatever its other commands charge.
- **So the funds come back.** Bulldoze then undo, and place then undo, leave
  the funds where they were, for buildings, roads, stops, power lines and
  pipes. Redo replays the edit at its own price.
- **Refused only for the net.** An undo is refused for funds when the city
  cannot pay the settled amount, which means the refund has been spent.
- **A refused batch is taken back whole.** Its take-back ran through the
  funds gates, so a city too poor to buy back a building the batch had
  bulldozed lost it; the gates now stand aside there too.

### Rush hour and service hours stay on the day the player sees (decided 2026-11-12)

A ground truth said the sky's clock never drives a sim rule, and two rules
read it: traffic's rush curve and a transit line's service span. The player
was asked which should give way and kept the clock on screen.

- **The rule names its two readers.** `[R-world-006]` now says the calendar
  runs dates, budgets and every monthly rule, the day the player sees runs
  the rush curve and the service span, and no third sim rule may read it.
- **Why not the calendar day.** It is 200 ticks, half a minute at the 1×
  button, against six minutes for the sky's day: on it traffic would swell
  and ebb twelve times in one sweep of the sky, and every fleet would pull
  out and go home as often.
- **Nothing in play changes.**
  [time-system.md](../world-sim/time-system.md) says which clock each rule
  reads, and the lighting document's day length is corrected to the 1×
  button's six minutes.

### The page keeps answering while a city grows at 4× (built 2026-11-10)

With a hundred blocks zoned at once and the speed at 4×, the page stopped
answering for a minute at a time and then ran out of memory. A CPU profile
showed why: the worker posted 53 snapshots a second, and each did work over
the whole city on the main thread.

- **A snapshot costs what it changed** `[R-render-037]`. The tiles a lot's
  drive crosses and each walker's path are kept per building and laid again
  only for the buildings a delta names, or for all when a road, a section or
  a junction control moves.
- **Pools start large.** Below about a thousand instances three compiles an
  instanced mesh's capacity into its shader, so every pool that doubled as
  the city grew linked a new program in the render loop: 280 in one run. No
  pool is now allocated for fewer than 2,048, and 64 first compiles remain.
- **Meshes are kept, not rebuilt.** Pedestrians, median trees, lamps and road
  furniture keep their meshes and set a live count.
- **The leak.** Ten renderers took a replaced mesh off the scene and never
  disposed it; the median trees did so on every junction change. That was the
  memory.
- **One snapshot a pump.** A pump of five ticks posts once, after its last
  tick: 20 snapshots a second at 4×, and nothing is lost, since every delta
  gathers in the worker until a post drains it.
- **Cheaper rebuilds.** A road chunk's forty lookups a tile are one index
  each; a lot's normals are laid once with the lot.
- **The gauge.** `tools/services-shots.mjs` grows its city at 4× again and
  fails on a read-back over its limit, on any page error and on one that
  never returns.

- **Vehicles glide.** They are interpolated over the measured time between
  snapshots, not a fixed tenth of a second: at 1× a car no longer moves for
  100 ms and stands for 200.

The longest read-back fell from 57 s to 1.1 s and the heap from 2.1 GB to
0.6 GB, against the 1 s target in
[performance-budget.md](../engineering/performance-budget.md#the-main-thread-while-a-city-grows).
